## Establishing a comprehensive test and quality strategy
## Prepared by:  Krisha Wadhwa

## Purpose:  A practical, risk-based proposal for establishing a scalable test and quality function.


## Executive summary

This repository presents my proposed approach to establishing a practical and effective quality strategy at MIMIRO. The goal is to improve product quality, protect data reliability, reduce risks, and support safe and efficient delivery.

## Core Quality Principles

- Quality is everyone's responsibility
Product teams are responsible for the quality of the changes they build. The Test Lead helps establish common practices, identify risks, and improve the overall quality process.

- Testing is based on risk
Critical data flows, business rules, integrations, and customer-facing features receive more testing because failures in these areas can have a greater impact.

- Find problems early
Automated tests provide fast feedback during development. Manual, exploratory, and acceptance testing help identify issues that automated tests may miss.

- Data quality is a priority
Data should be complete, valid, consistent, unique where required, accurate where it can be verified, and available on time.

- Release decisions are based on evidence
Before release, the team reviews test results, open defects, operational readiness, and remaining risks against agreed release criteria.

- Continuous improvement
Quality metrics, production incidents, and testing results are used to identify improvements. Targets are set after understanding the current situation, rather than choosing arbitrary numbers.

## Proposed Approach

The proposed approach is divided into three stages:

1. First 30 days: Understand the current quality process, identify risks, and agree on priorities.

2. Within 60 days: Improve critical tests, strengthen data quality checks, and introduce basic quality metrics.

3. Within 90 days: Establish a consistent testing process, review results, and create a longer-term improvement roadmap.


## Assumptions

This is a proposed baseline based on the information available in the assignment. The final approach should be agreed with MIMIRO management and development teams after reviewing the existing systems, processes, tools, resources, and business risks.
